## Per-Docket Summary

### DMA Docket 1658 — "Consolidated Molybdenum Inc. Holdings in Buena Vista District"
**Page count:** 244 pages | **Report by:** Olin M. Sprague, Professional Engineer, Bremerton WA  
**Date range:** ~1948–1951 | **Status:** APPROVED initially; later actions noted

**Content & Geology:**
- **Host rock:** Quartz veins (89 mentions) in granite (25 mentions), with pegmatoid character
- **Vein description:** 181 mentions of "vein" across the document; range from "undiscernible veinlets to six-inch-wide veins"
- **Ore mineralogy:** Molybdenite (MoS₂), with pegmatite-hosted quartz-feldspar; limited gold/silver potential
- **Mineralization style:** Hydrothermal alteration zones with iron staining (limonite)
- **Exploration notes:** 
  - Examination included mapping and sampling of main vein system
  - Quartz veins strike NW–SE; dip steep; locally offset by faults
  - Pegmatites noted as irregular in structure with variable depth distribution
  - Depth uncertainty: "no mine starting on a productive Pegmatite is likely to reach a horizon too deep" — a cautionary note on unexplored extensions

**Assay Data (Molybdenum Focus):**
- **Grade patterns:** 0.35–0.55% copper (where present); **0.01–0.30% molybdenum**
- **Gold/Silver:** Negligible (0 oz Au/ton; trace silver 0.1–0.2 oz/ton where tested)
- **Economic observation:** "Approximately 50% of the molybdenum produced is utilized in the making of alloys of iron and steel."

**Approval & Loan Status:**
- Approved for DMA assistance; loan documented
- Financial records show capitalization of $154,400.00 issued stock and deferred development costs of $2,227.54
- Multiple progress reports filed through 1950–1951

**Key Limitation:** A 244-page report on what appears to be a single vein system suggests either extensive pegmatite mapping and geochemical testing, or multiple subsidiary claims or vein groups within the property.

### DMA Docket 1744 — "Quartz Crystal Exploration (Part A) + Molybdenum Continuation (Part B)"
**Page count:** 73 pages (Part A ~36 pages, Part B ~37 pages) | **Docket date:** Filed ~1950–1952

**Part A — Quartz Crystal Application (DENIED)**
- **Location:** Section 27, T. 25 N, R. 10 E, King County (26 miles NE of North Bend)
- **Applicant:** Consolidated Molybdenum Inc.
- **Target commodity:** Radio-grade quartz crystals
- **Status:** **DENIED** by DMEA
- **Reason:** Ten samples of quartz crystals submitted to Star Signal Laboratory, Port Portsmouth, NJ could not be classified as radio-grade
- **Conclusion:** "Your application is therefore denied."
- **Implication:** Molybdenum company attempted diversification into strategic quartz; supply insufficient quality

**Part B — Molybdenum Correspondence & Engineering Notes**
- Continued technical correspondence on the molybdenum vein system
- Balance sheet (Dec. 31, 1950): $219.31 cash, $600 accounts receivable; total assets $56,062.60
- McMillin (Economic Geologist) assessment suggests some optimism but uncertainty about testing protocol

**Historical Timing Clue:** A Feb. 18, 1952 phone conversation puts this phase at ~mid-1952

### DMEA Docket 3557 — "Devils Canyon Mining Company Copper–Molybdenum Exploration (1951–1954)"
**Page count:** 45 pages | **Application date:** ~December 1951 | **Final decision:** January–February 1952 | **Status:** **DENIED**

**Applicant & Operations:**
- **Company:** Devils Canyon Mining Company
- **Partners/Operators:** Vernon T. Osterberg, George C. Selfridge
- **Consultants:** Edwin Saurers, Carl Johnston (attorney)
- **Property:** 10 unpatented claims in Sections 26–27, T. 25 N., R. 10 E., W.M., Buena Vista District, King County, WA

**Mineral Target:** **Copper–Molybdenum** (shift from molybdenum-primary in earlier dockets)

**Geological Basis:**
Application was "based upon the existence of weak fracture zones in granite." This represents a porphyry copper or fracture-zone disseminated mineralization model, distinct from the vein model in DMA reports.

**Exploration Work Performed:**
- Field examination by Puffett, November 23, 1951
- Examination duration: one full day; 3 hours spent on property examination, mapping, and sampling
- Work included: surface mapping, sample collection from mineralized zones

**Critical Assay Results:**

| Sample Description | Au (oz/ton) | Ag (oz/ton) | Cu (%) | Mo (%) |
|-------------------|------------|-----------|--------|---------|
| **Width 1.5–4.0 ft** | 0.0 | 0.1–0.2 | 0.35–0.55 | 0.01–0.03 |
| **Mineralized zone #1** | 0.0 | 0.1–0.11 | 0.35–0.50 | — to 0.302 |
| **Highest grade piece** | 0.0 | 0.11 | 0.50 | **0.302** |
| **Short adit face** | 0.0 | 0.1 | 0.35 | 0.015 |
| **Second occurrence** | 0.0 | 0.2 | 0.55 | 0.010–0.015 |

**Key Observations:**
- **NO GOLD** in any sample
- **Silver trace:** 0.1–0.2 oz/ton (marginal for precious metal credit)
- **Copper:** 0.35–0.55% — sub-economic for 1950s porphyry standards
- **Molybdenum:** 0.01–0.30%, with 0.302% max — very low for molybdenum primary exploration
- **Mineralization style:** "Disseminated ore minerals" in weak fracture zones; "sparse amount" of exposure

**Field Team Conclusions (Puffett, Nov. 23, 1951):**
- "Sampling and mapping of the best showings indicated that only very small tonnage of material, containing a few tenths of a percent of copper, and minor molybdenum, could be expected."
- "Core drilling and tunnelling are imperative for further examination."
- **Final recommendation:** **Denial of DMEA assistance**
  - "The sparse amount of disseminated ore minerals exposed on the applicant's property does not suggest that further exploration is justified."
  - "The amount of mineralization and the general character of the deposit is not sufficiently promising to justify DMEA assistance."

**DMEA Final Decision (January 1952):**
- "The examination appears to have been made with care, and the information reported fully justifies the recommended denial."
- Application **DENIED**

## Synthesis: Property Geology, Ore Body, Production Potential

### Geological Evolution Across Four Dockets

**DMA 1658/1744/1746 Model (Consolidated Molybdenum, 1948–1951):**
- **Host:** Pegmatitic granite with quartz-feldspar composition
- **Mineralization:** Discrete quartz veins (NW–SE strike, steep dip) with molybdenite (MoS₂) dissemination
- **Dimensions:** Veins 0.1–6 inches wide; multiple parallel veinlets; irregular depth distribution
- **Grade (molybdenum):** 0.01–0.30%, with local higher-grade concentrates tested
- **Alteration:** Limonite (iron oxide staining); some pegmatoid character

**DMEA 3557 Model (Devils Canyon Mining Co., 1951–1954):**
- **Host:** Granite with weak fracture zones
- **Mineralization:** Disseminated copper–molybdenum in fractures (porphyry-style, not discrete veins)
- **Grade:** 0.35–0.55% Cu, 0.01–0.30% Mo, 0.1–0.2 oz/ton Ag, **0.0 Au**
- **Tonnage:** "Sparse" and "small tonnage"; insufficient to demonstrate economic potential
- **Examiner's assessment:** Weak fracture zones; limited exposure; deposit "not sufficiently promising"

### Production Potential Assessment

**As of 1951–1954:**

| Criterion | Assessment | Evidence |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Ore Grade** | **SUB-ECONOMIC** | Cu 0.35–0.55%, Mo 0.01–0.30%, Ag trace, Au nil — below typical 1950s standards |
| **Tonnage** | **UNKNOWN / SPARSE** | DMA reports vague; DMEA explicitly notes "sparse…small tonnage" |
| **Drilling Evidence** | **MINIMAL** | No drill intercepts documented; DMEA notes drilling "imperative" but not completed |
| **Vein Continuity** | **UNCERTAIN** | Pegmatites noted as "irregular"; depth range "great"; no mine workings recorded |
| **Historic Production** | **NONE RECORDED** | DMA 1746: "The past history of the property is not recorded in the literature." |
| **Feasibility** | **NOT DEMONSTRATED** | McMillin (1950) expressed hope bulk sampling might show promise; Puffett (1951) concluded deposit not promising |

### Why Both Applications Were Denied or Shelved

1. **DMA 1658–1746 (Molybdenum):** Formally denied November 23, 1951. Bulk sampling plan never executed. Company's balance sheet (1950) shows minimal cash ($219), suggesting capital constraints.

2. **DMEA 3557 (Devils Canyon, Copper–Molybdenum):** Denied January–February 1952 because sparse, disseminated mineralization did not meet threshold for federal cost-sharing. Further drilling was deemed "imperative" but not recommended for federal funding.

3. **No Subsequent Production Records:** Given that Buena Vista is a recognized historical gold district but Devils Canyon is not prominently mentioned in subsequent WA mining records, the property was likely abandoned by mid-1950s.
