## Snoqualmie Batholith (Priority Section)

### Composition & Lithology
The Snoqualmie Granodiorite (Snoqualmie Batholith) in King County consists of **multiple granitoid phases**:
- Granodiorite, quartz diorite, diorite, granite (dominant)
- Aplite, intrusive breccia
- Dacite porphyry, tonalite, gabbro (subordinate)
- Compositions vary: **less calcic west-to-east** (Allentown–Tukwila area = porphyritic basalt/andesite; foothills = andesite porphyry; Cascades = dacite/rhyolite/diorite/granodiorite)

**Total outcrop area in King County:** ~250 square miles, mostly north of Snoqualmie Pass and east of a line between Chester Morse Lake and Index.

**Thickness:** Unknown but exceeds 20,000 feet.

### Age
- **Miocene, specifically 17 million years** (Lipson et al. 1961; potassium-argon method)
- Tertiary intrusion (not older Mesozoic Mount Stuart Granodiorite of NE county corner)

### Contact Aureole & Mineralization Relationship
- **Critical finding:** "A definite relation exists between the metallic ore deposits of the county and the batholithic rocks; this is shown on Figure 12."
- Mineral deposits **associated with Tertiary granitoid rocks but NOT with older Mesozoic Mount Stuart Granodiorite**
- Limestone pods intruded by Snoqualmie Batholith at Grotto–Snoqualmie Pass contact show **alteration, recrystallization, and SKARN ZONES along contact** (Danner 1966, p. 359–386)
- Folding absent in granodiorite proper; however, **batholithic mass plunges southward from alpine highs in the north, passing beneath Tertiary volcanic rocks at Snoqualmie Pass** — may reflect differential uplift
- Origin: **Both by granitization of older rocks AND by intrusion of magmatic mass into older rocks**

### Emplacement Structures & Ore Control
- **Shear zones:** "Several large shear zones cutting the granodiorite in the Money Creek–Miller River–Lennox Creek area" (p. 5042) — **relative movement and relation to ore bodies not studied**
- **Breccia pipes:** Common in northeastern part of county; **often serve as loci for ore mineralization**
- **Jointing:** Abundant throughout granitoid rocks; usually more than one joint set at any given locality
- **Fault hard-rock expression:** "In batholithic rocks they are particularly hard to recognize, and it is in those rocks that they are most important in the control of ore bodies"

## King County Mining Districts

### District Boundaries (Hodges 1897 as redefined by Livingston)

#### **Money Creek District**
- **Boundary:** Line trending west from Baring along King–Snohomish County line to Mount Index; southeast to Lennox Mountain and Coney Lake along drainage divide (Money Creek vs. Tait and North Fork Snoqualmie); northeast along divide (Money Creek vs. Miller River) to confluence with Skykomish River; then west along Skykomish to Baring
- **Key drainages:** Money Creek, Tye River, Skykomish River upper reaches
- **Elevation range:** ~3,600 ft (headwaters) to lower elevations at confluences
- **Host rocks:** Snoqualmie Granodiorite, minor Tertiary volcanics

#### **Miller River District**
- **Boundary:** Highway from Stevens Pass to confluence of Miller and Skykomish Rivers; southwestward along west border of basin (Miller and West Fork Miller River) to ~1 mile west of Dream Lake; eastward along divide (Snoqualmie River vs. tributary streams) to Stevens Pass
- **Key drainages:** Miller River, West Fork Miller River
- **Elevation range:** 700 ft (valley floor near confluence) to ~5,200 ft (upper basin)
- **Host rocks:** Snoqualmie Granodiorite with shear zones and breccia pipes

#### **Buena Vista District**
- **Boundary:** West along King–Snohomish County line from Mount Index to NE corner of T. 26 N., R. 9 E.; south toward Fuller Mountain; east along drainage divides (Hancock/Calligan Creeks; Taylor/Lennox Rivers) to ~1 mile west of Dream Lake; north along divide (Lennox Creek vs. West Fork Miller River) to Lennox Mountain; NW to Mount Index
- **Host rocks:** Snoqualmie Granodiorite on margins; Tertiary volcanic rocks in center

#### **Snoqualmie District**
- **Boundary:** North—drainage divide from Mount Hinman to Fuller Mountain; east—Cascade crest (Mount Hinman to Tinkham Peak); south—divide (Snoqualmie–Cedar Rivers, Tinkham Peak to Rattlesnake Mountain); west—bend around Rattlesnake Mountain to Fuller Mountain
- **Host rocks:** Snoqualmie Granodiorite; Tertiary volcanics; pre-Tertiary basement near crest

#### **Cedar River District**
- **Boundary:** East—Cascade crest (Meadow Mountain to Tinkham Peak); north—divide (Cedar–Snoqualmie Rivers); south/west—divide from crest to Cedar Butte and west

## Stratigraphy: Delta vs. Existing `wa-tertiary-stratigraphy.md` Skill

### New Units Introduced (King County specific)

1. **Swauk Formation (Eocene–Oligocene?)**
   - Arkosic sandstone, conglomerate, shale in continuous belt from Beckler River to Deception Pass
   - Minor hornfelsing near igneous contacts
   - Age: younger than Paleozoic, older than Mount Stuart (pre-Mesozoic), but exact placement within Eocene–Miocene spectrum unclear in Livingston
   - **Skill update:** Mention in context of pre-Miocene basement to Snoqualmie Batholith

2. **Tiger Mountain Formation**
   - TOC reference (p. 29); appears in Renton–Cedar Mountain area
   - No detailed description given in main text; likely Eocene–Oligocene, non-marine or lacustrine
   - **Skill reference:** Tertiary lowland sequence (westside Puget Lowlands)

3. **Tukwila Formation**
   - Near Seattle lowlands, Black River, Riverton quarries
   - Silica deposits; clay
   - Likely Eocene–Oligocene non-marine

4. **Renton Formation**
   - Coal measures (known from `wa-eocene-coal-fields` skill); expanded detail in Livingston
   - Folded NW in Newcastle Hills–Renton area
   - Associated with Hammer Bluff Formation (below)
   - **Confirm:** Newcastle, Renton, Green River coal seams map into this unit

5. **Hammer Bluff Formation**
   - Clay, silts; superior aggregate properties documented
   - Post-coal, likely Eocene–Oligocene
   - Renton area exploited for clay and silica quarries

6. **Guye Formation**
   - Snoqualmie Pass area; detailed by Foster (1955)
   - Clastic sequence; no gold indication

7. **Naches Formation**
   - Southern Cascades; sparse detail in Livingston
   - Likely volcanic or volcaniclastic

8. **Raging River Formation**
   - Quarry noted; clastic or volcaniclastic
   - Likely mid-Tertiary

9. **Mt. Catherine Rhyolite**
   - Rhyolitic flow or dome; appears south of pass
   - Likely Eocene–Miocene extrusive

10. **Red Mountain Formation (Plummer 1964, cited but unpublished)**
    - Near headwaters of Money Creek
    - Statically metamorphosed conglomerate, argillaceous sandstone, hornfelsed argillite
    - Elastic material partly from Paleozoic sources (Baring–Grotto analogs)
    - **Age unknown; younger than Paleozoic, pre-Miocene(?)**
    - **Novel:** No published correlation found as of 1969
    - **Mineralization potential:** Not explicitly stated; exotic conglomerate suggests proximal to active tectonism

### Existing Skill Alignment
- **`wa-tertiary-stratigraphy.md`** focuses on Weaver (1916) west-of-Cascades Eocene–Oligocene coal measures and associated strata (Newcastle, Renton, Green River, Centralia)

## Structure (Seattle Fault, Rattlesnake Mountain, Fold Systems)

### Structural Grains
1. **Northwest-trending grain (Oligocene, Calkins Range)**
   - Established during Oligocene by earth stresses folding Oligocene and older rocks
   - Controls modern river courses (Skykomish, Miller River, Money Creek trend NW)
   - **Survives in shear zones in granodiorite** — "Large, pronounced northwest-trending shear zones in the plutonic rocks indicate that this system may still be active"

2. **North-south grain (late Pliocene to early Pleistocene, Cascade Range arch)**
   - Main Cascade uplift axis
   - Superimposed minor folds obliterate main axis
   - Pre-existing NW structures reinforced during uplift

### Specific Structures in Mineralization
- **Money Creek–Miller River–Lennox Creek shear zone system:** Multiple large shears cutting granodiorite; relative movement **not studied** (p. 5042)
- **Breccia pipes:** NE portion of county; **ore loci**
- **Rattlesnake Mountain:** Referenced as boundary marker in Snoqualmie district; fold axis implied but not detailed
- **Denny Formation (Snoqualmie Pass):** Graywacke/breccia/shale with northerly strike and steep dips; hornfelsed at granodiorite contact

### Seattle Fault
- **Not explicitly named in Livingston**; bulletin predates major Seattle Fault delineation
- Fold systems in Renton–Newcastle Hills (NW trend) and Green River area (N-NE trend) hint at deeper fault control, but not developed
