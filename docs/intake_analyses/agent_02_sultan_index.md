## Index District: Key Findings

### Stratigraphy (Formations)

**Gunn Peak Formation** (Pre-Cretaceous, Carboniferous–Triassic/Jurassic metamorphic)
- Quartzite, slate, crystalline limestone, chert, schist, metavolcanics
- ~16 sq mi in SE district; intensely folded/faulted; layers contorted
- **Higher Au/Ag content than granodiorite veins** (pyrite association)
- Contact zone minerals: garnet, epidote, hornblende

**Index Granodiorite** (Late Jurassic pre-Tertiary, TYPE LOCALITY)
- Quartz diorite/granodiorite batholith; plagioclase (andesine)-rich, hornblende, biotite
- Hosts majority of district; supports larger, higher-grade Cu veins
- Contact with Gunn Peak sharp, often vertical; metamorphic rocks show gneissoid banding

**Tertiary Units** (Eocene–Miocene)
- Howard Arkose: 500–700 ft coarse conglomerate, arkosic (Conglomerate Point area)
- West Index Andesitic Series: 2,500–3,000 ft intercalated breccia/lavas (Mount West Index, Mount Persis)
- Both unconformable; locally Cu-stained

### Mines Summary
**20+ developed properties documented:**
- Sunset, Non-Pareil, Ethel Consolidated, Copper Bell (major)
- Index Bornite, Index Peacock, Gunn Peak, Helena, North Star, Index Independent, Buckeye (active)
- Lost Creek, Uncle Sam, Florence-Rae, Bitter Creek, Calumet, Kittanning, Merchant, Homestead, Co-operative (lesser)

**Key Statistics:**
- Primary commodity: Copper (chalcopyrite, bornite)
- Highest Au/Ag: Lost Creek samples (2.5–5 oz Ag/ton, 0.2 oz Au/ton) in metamorphic quartzite/pyrite veins
- Production: ~10 carloads Sunset ore (5% Cu) to Tacoma smelter; total district output low
- Decline: Post-1902 due to Cu price collapse (12¢/lb) and transport costs

### Structural Control (CRITICAL FOR structure/gold.md)

**Vein Geometry:**
- **Arcuate distribution:** N 75° W (east) → E–W (center) → N 45°–10° E (west); **convex to north**
- **Dip:** 70°–90° to S/SE/SW (nearly vertical)
- **Minor fractures:** ~40° to NE/N/NW; intersect majors at ~90°
- **Ore lenses:** Lens-shaped within fissure plane; unpredictable with depth; continue >1,000 ft below sea level

**Interpretation:**
- Late Pliocene mountain-building uplift (SE–NW axial warp)
- Cascades dome deformation; possibly NW–SE fault-block tilting
- Skykomish valley drainage adjustment to structural warp

**Host-Rock Influence:**
- Granodiorite: Regular, persistent fissures; larger ore bodies
- Metamorphic: Intersecting small fractures; network impregnation; hard host
- Contact zones: Enhanced mineralization (garnet, epidote, Cu enrichment)
