## Conconully Mining District Findings

### District Overview
- **Location:** Okanogan County, Washington (central-eastern WA)
- **Primary commodity:** Silver (with gold, copper, lead as byproducts)
- **Principal host rocks:** Granodiorite of Similkameen batholith (west); metamorphic rocks (schists, gneisses) of Salmon Creek formation (east). Contact is NW-trending with sharp intrusive and transitional migmatitic zones.
- **Deposit type:** Vein-hosted, typically in fault/shear zones; minor pegmatitic/aplitic dikes.
- **Era of activity:** 1889–1964, with peak production 1889–1893 and secondary peaks 1937–1939 and 1958–1964.

### District Production
- **Total estimated production:** ~$350,000 (conservative; incomplete historical records)
- **Peak years:** 1889–1893: $200,000 combined from Arlington, First Thought, Last Chance, Fourth of July mines (mostly silver)
- **Secondary peak:** 1937–1939: Arlington mine shipped silver-copper concentrates valued at $71,683
- **Final production:** 1958–1964: ~$12,000 from Fourth of July mine (silver ore)
- **Note:** Records incomplete; actual values likely higher

### Lithology
**Granodiorite:** Similkameen batholith; variants = hornblende, biotite, porphyritic granodiorite; quartz diorite and quartz monzonite (Loup Loup granodiorite) on Little Peacock Mt./Ruby Hill (Menzer 1964).

**Metamorphic rocks:** Salmon Creek schists/gneisses—pelitic quartzitic schist, mica schist, amphibolitic schist, calc-silicate marble, quartz diorite gneiss, granulite, amphibolite. Paleozoic sediments/volcanics metamorphosed Jurassic orogeny, later intruded late Mesozoic granites.

**Pegmatite/aplite:** Dikelike masses in granodiorite/metamorphic; most barren; Wheeler mine pegmatite carries sparse molybdenite.

**Ore minerals (inferred):** Quartz veins with sulfides (pyrite, chalcopyrite, galena, sphalerite, arsenopyrite); silver as native + complex sulfides.

**Structure:** Veins in steeply dipping faults/shear zones; contact granodiorite-metamorphic NW-trending, sharp to gradational (migmatitic).

## Apex Mine (Money Creek Mining District, King County)

### Location & Geology
- **County:** King County
- **District:** Money Creek Mining District
- **USGS coords:** SW¼SW¼ sec. 34, T26N R10E
- **Elevation:** 3800 ft (glacial cirque)
- **Host:** Quartz vein 2–6 ft wide, >1000 ft long in granodiorite (Snoqualmie batholith)
- **Ore:** Gold in solid solution with arsenopyrite; galena/sphalerite lenses (Pb, Ag)
- **Ore grades (1936):** 91 samples averaged **1.2 oz Au/ton**, **7 oz Ag/ton**; smelter returns 1920–1934 confirm Au/Ag plus sporadic Pb (6%) and As (21–38%)

### Mine Workings & Infrastructure
- **Development:** 2950 ft haulage (5 adits); ~5000 ft stope/raise additional
- **Adits:** Adit 2 (3940 ft, open); Adit 3 (3800 ft, caved); Adit 4 (main producer, Milwaukee Ck canyon)
- **Tramway:** 3200-ft aerial jig-back (Adit 4 → mill) + narrow-gauge rail to Miller R. → Tacoma smelter
- **Power:** 1650-ft wooden flume (22") from Lake Elizabeth → Pelton wheel
- **Mill:** Water-wash tables; 75-ton flotation added 1938 (used only 1938–1940)

### Production & History
- **Discovery:** 1889
- **Closure:** 1941 (War Production Board L-208)
- **Total:** ~$300,000 at historic prices
- **Last operator:** Apex Gold Mines Inc. (dissolved 1943)
- **Current:** Joe B. Cashman (Cleopatra Mining Co.), 10 active lode claims

### Water Quality
- **AMD:** Not actively discharging (2001), standing water in Adit 2 (pH 5.0)
- **Metals:** Water samples exceeded WAC standards (As, Pb, Zn); As at Adit 2 >15 μg/L; Money Cr. confluence ≤15 μg/L (at state standard)
- **Mineralization:** Milwaukee Cr. flows through vein; difficult to partition natural vs. mining-related metals

## Mystery and Justice Mines (Monte Cristo Mining District, Snohomish Co.)

### Location & Overview
- **County:** Snohomish County
- **District:** Monte Cristo Mining District (60 mi E of Everett)
- **Coords:** SE¼ sec. 22, SW¼ sec. 23, T22N R11E
- **Elevation:** 3000–5000 ft
- **Status:** Henry M. Jackson Wilderness Area; USFS + private; 9 patented claims transferred USFS 1994

### History
- **Discovery:** 1889 (Pearsall, Seventysix Gulch); 1890 (Wilmans/Peabody, Pride vein)
- **Financing:** John D. Rockefeller (via Colby-Hoyt), 1891
- **Rail & smelter:** E&MCR + smelter at Lowell completed 1893
- **Peak:** Summer 1897 (310k tons; 90% from Mystery/Justice)
- **Ownership:** Rockefeller → Guggenheim/ASARCO 1903 → Wilmans 1905 → Silverman 1906 → Receivership 1907
- **Closure drivers:** E&MCR washouts (1896–1897), rising As at depth, continued rail damage; last serious mining 1915

### Nomenclature
Complex of interconnected mines:
- **Mystery workings (west Mystery Hill):** Main haulage via Mystery adit 3 (summer 1896)
- **Glacier Basin (east, connected to Mystery):** Pride of the Woods, Pride of the Mtn. (1100-ft aerial tram), New Discovery (Adits 1–2)
- **Golden Cord:** 3 adits on Wilmon Pk. slopes, connected to Justice via raise
- **Justice:** Justice tunnel (adit 3 in historic use), lowest workings

### Geology
**Host:** Granodiorite + andesite with near-vertical shear zones
**Ore lenses:** 100–300 ft long, 1–15 ft wide

**Ore minerals:**
- Shallow (<50–250 ft): Galena, sphalerite, chalcopyrite abundant
- Deep (>50–250 ft): Arsenopyrite, pyrite; galena nonexistent; some Cu/Zn persist
- Minor: Stibnite, jamesonite, realgar

### Production & Infrastructure
- **Total district:** 310k tons
- **Mystery/Justice share:** ~90%
- **Peak:** 1897
- **Concentrator:** United Concentration gravity mill at Monte Cristo townsite
- **Shipping:** Aerial tram → concentrator → rail → smelter (Lowell or external)

### Water Quality & AMD
- **AMD:** Documented at Mystery adit 3 (red iron staining visible)
- **As mobility:** Increasing As at depth; arsenopyrite/realgar/jamesonite abundant → high As in drainage
- **Benthic impacts:** Fish habitat assessment 2002
