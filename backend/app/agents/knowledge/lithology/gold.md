# Lithology Agent — Gold Knowledge Base (Washington State)

You are scoring grid cells in Washington state for gold mineralization potential based on lithology. This reference contains everything you need to make informed, defensible scoring decisions.

---

## Washington's Gold-Bearing Geological Provinces

Washington has distinct geological provinces with very different gold potential. Use this to establish baseline expectations before evaluating individual rock units.

**Okanogan Highlands / NE Washington Grabens** (Ferry and Okanogan Counties) — VERY HIGH potential. Washington's most productive gold province. Four Eocene grabens contain >98% of the Eocene volcaniclastic rocks and host virtually all epithermal gold: **Republic Graben**, **Toroda Creek Graben**, **Keller Graben**, and **First Thought Graben**. Sanpoil Volcanics and Klondike Mountain Formation host all 50 known epithermal training sites (USGS OF01-501). Total epithermal production: 3+ Moz Au through 1997. Only 4.6% of the NE Washington study area is classified as "favorable" by WofE posterior probability, but this 4.6% contains 49 of 50 known deposits. Quaternary glacial till covers parts of the district and can mask favorable lithology (cf. Kettle mine).

**North Cascades Metamorphic Core** (central-northern WA) — HIGH potential in shear zones. Proterozoic metasediments (Index Schist, Chiwaukum Schist) host orogenic gold veins at Blewett (~400 koz), Monte Cristo (~400 koz), and Wenatchee (~200 koz). Gold occurs in steeply-dipping quartz veins along NW-SE foliation and NE cross-fractures, especially at schist-amphibolite contacts.

**Wenatchee / Cascade Transition Zone** (Chelan County) — MODERATE potential. Mix of metamorphic basement and Cretaceous intrusions. Orogenic veins in Swakane Gneiss and Chiwaukum Schist, some contact mineralization near Wenatchee Pluton.

**Liberty / Swauk District** (Kittitas County) — MODERATE for placer, LOW for lode. Gold concentrated in Eocene Swauk Formation fluvial conglomerates. Primary lode deposits are small.

**Blue Mountains / NE Corner** (Pend Oreille County) — MODERATE. Similar orogenic vein style to North Cascades but less explored. Archean gneiss and Proterozoic metasediments.

**Metaline District** (Pend Oreille County) — LOW-MODERATE for gold. Primarily a base-metal (Pb-Zn-Ag) skarn district in Metaline Formation carbonates. Gold occurs as a byproduct.

**West of Cascade Crest** — LOW to NEGLIGIBLE. Accretionary complex rocks, younger Tertiary volcanics, and thick sedimentary cover. Almost no historic gold production. The Cascade crest is a fundamental structural divide — gold districts are overwhelmingly on the east side.

**Columbia Plateau** (eastern WA) — NEGLIGIBLE. Covered by 1000+ m of Miocene Columbia River Basalt Group (CRBG). No gold in basalt itself, but it may conceal mineralized basement. CRBG is a strong negative indicator for surface prospecting.

## Gold Deposit Types in Washington

Washington hosts three deposit types relevant to lithology scoring. Each has different favorable host rocks.

### 1. Epithermal (Low-Sulfidation) — Republic Type
- **Where**: Republic district; anywhere Eocene calc-alkaline volcanics occur in graben settings (Republic, Toroda Creek, Keller, First Thought grabens)
- **Host rocks**: Andesite, tuff, pyroclastic rocks (Sanpoil Volcanics, Klondike Mountain Formation)
- **Temperature**: 150–300°C, shallow depth (<1500 m)
- **Alteration**: Adularia + sericite + chalcedonic quartz. Silica flooding in high-grade zones (>90% quartz by volume). Pale pink to white silicified rocks.
- **Sulfides**: Low pyrite (<5%), minor arsenopyrite, sphalerite, galena
- **Grades**: 2–10 g/t Au; Au:Ag ratio 1:5 to 1:50
- **Total epithermal production**: 3+ million oz Au through 1997 (USGS OF01-501, 50 training sites)
- **Key structural control**: NW-to-NNE oriented normal faults (optimum buffer 1,700 m per WofE analysis); fault intersections and dilatational bends are high-priority targets
- **Pathfinder elements**: Hg (>100 ppb anomalous), Sb, As, Se
- **Placer gold association**: 67 documented placer gold sites in NE Washington. Spatial correlation with epithermal deposits is significant within 4,000 m buffer (WofE optimum distance). Placer gold downstream of epithermal systems indicates active erosion of mineralized rock.

### 2. Orogenic / Mesothermal — Blewett, Monte Cristo Type
- **Where**: North Cascades metamorphic core, east of Cascade crest
- **Host rocks**: Greenschist-to-lower-amphibolite facies metamorphic rocks — especially mica schist (Index Schist, Chiwaukum Schist), interlayered amphibolite, metasandstone
- **Temperature**: 300–400°C, deeper crustal levels (3–15 km paleodepth)
- **Alteration**: Sericite + silicification + carbonatization halos (0.5–5 m wide), pyritic envelope. Subtle compared to epithermal — narrow halos, not self-advertising.
- **Sulfides**: Pyrite (dominant), arsenopyrite (diagnostic), minor chalcopyrite
- **Grades**: 1–5 g/t Au, larger tonnages than epithermal
- **Key structural control**: Steeply-dipping quartz veins in shear zones, NW-SE foliation cross-cut by NE fractures. "Saddle reef" geometry in folded units. Gold concentrates at schist-amphibolite lithologic contacts (competency contrast).
- **Critical insight**: The optimal host is a heterolithic sequence where strong layers (metasandstone, amphibolite) alternate with weak layers (pelite, phyllite), creating sustained fracture permeability during deformation.
- **Pathfinder elements**: As (100–1000 ppm in mineralized rock), Au, Sb, W

### 3. Skarn — Metaline Type
- **Where**: Metaline district; locally at Slate Creek and other pluton-carbonate contacts
- **Host rocks**: Marble, calc-silicate rocks, Metaline Formation carbonates — all must be within ~500 m of an intrusion (granodiorite, monzonite)
- **Temperature**: 300–600°C
- **Alteration**: Garnet-pyroxene-magnetite (prograde); epidote-chlorite-calcite + sulfides (retrograde). Gold concentrates in the retrograde phase.
- **Grades**: Low (0.1–0.5 g/t Au) but consistent; copper is usually the primary commodity
- **Key control**: Contact geometry. Steep contacts (>70°) allow higher fluid flux and are more favorable than gentle contacts.

## USGS Weights-of-Evidence Analysis: Quantitative Lithologic Predictors (OF01-501)

The USGS conducted a rigorous weights-of-evidence (WofE) GIS analysis across a 222 km × 277 km area of NE Washington (Boleneus et al., 2001). This study used 50 epithermal gold training sites and measured the spatial correlation between lithologic units and gold occurrence. The results below are **quantitative, statistically validated predictors** — use them to calibrate your scores.

### Contrast Value Interpretation Scale
| Contrast Value | Prediction Level | Approximate Probability |
|---|---|---|
| 0–0.5 | Mildly predictive | — |
| 0.5–1.0 | Moderately predictive | ~0.50–0.75 |
| 1.0–2.0 | Strongly predictive | ~0.75–0.88 |
| >2.0 | Extremely predictive | >0.88 |

A studentized contrast value ≥2.0 indicates ~98% confidence level.

### Favorable Lithologic Units (with 150 m buffer)

These are the ONLY lithologic units that showed positive spatial correlation with epithermal gold in the WofE analysis. All other units ranked "Outside" (zero training sites):

| Unit Code | Formation | Contrast | Training Sites | Area (km²) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Eck** | Klondike Mountain Fm — volcaniclastic rocks | **4.55** | 4 | 26.3 | Highest predictive power. Volcaniclastic facies of Klondike Fm. |
| **Evkct** | Klondike Mountain Fm — conglomerate & tuffs | **3.62** | 5 | 43.1 | Very high. Coarse volcaniclastic + pyroclastic mix. |
| **Evst** | Sanpoil Volcanics — tuffs | **3.42** | 5 | 50.6 | Very high. Pyroclastic facies of Sanpoil Volcanics. |
| **Evsf** | Sanpoil Volcanics — flows | **3.21** | 30 | 302.5 | Very high. Largest host by area. 30 of 50 sites — the dominant host rock. |
| **Evkf** | Klondike Mountain Fm — flows | **2.56** | 3 | 45.3 | Extremely predictive. Lava flows of the Klondike Fm. |
| **Eco** | O'Brien Creek Formation — metasediments | **1.96** | 2 | 35.2 | Strongly predictive. Basement metasediments underlying Eocene volcanics. |

**Key insight**: Sanpoil flows (Evsf) host 30 of 50 known epithermal deposits — 60% of all training sites — and are the single most important lithologic unit for gold in Washington. However, Klondike Mountain Formation volcaniclastics (Eck) have the *highest* contrast value, meaning they are the most spatially concentrated predictor per unit area.

### Non-Predictive Units (complete exclusion)

The WofE analysis tested 150+ lithologic units. Every unit not listed above — including all pre-Eocene metamorphic rocks, Cretaceous intrusives, Paleozoic carbonates, Mesozoic sediments, and Quaternary deposits — had **zero training sites** and ranked "Outside." This rigorously confirms that epithermal gold in NE Washington is exclusively hosted by Eocene volcanic/volcaniclastic rocks and their immediate basement (O'Brien Creek Fm).

Notable units with zero correlation: Eocene intrusives (Eig, Eimd, Eiqm — >580 km² combined, 0 sites), Quaternary deposits, all granitic rocks (Kigd — 1,289 km², 0 sites), all Proterozoic basement.

### Tract Classification (Posterior Probability)

The WofE model classifies the study area into tracts based on integrated posterior probability:

| Tract | Threshold | % of Study Area | Training Sites | Score Guidance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Favorable** | P > 0.024 | 4.6% | 49 of 50 | Score 0.70–0.95. Restricted to Republic, Toroda Creek, and Keller grabens where Eocene Klondike Fm and Sanpoil Volcanics outcrop. |
| **Permissive** | P > 0.000167 | 3.4% | 1 (Kettle mine — buried under Quaternary) | Score 0.35–0.65. Fringe areas with partial predictor overlap. |
| **Non-permissive** | P < 0.000167 | 92% | 0 | Score 0.00–0.30. Lacking all three predictor themes. |

**The Kettle mine exception**: One training site (the Kettle gold mine, a large producer) falls in the permissive rather than favorable tract because it is buried beneath Quaternary deposits. This demonstrates that cover can mask favorable lithology — note this when evaluating cells with Quaternary cover over Eocene volcanic terrain.

### Structural Predictor: NW-to-NNE Normal Faults

Normal faults oriented 345°–030° show optimum spatial correlation with epithermal gold at a 1,700 m buffer distance. These are the graben-bounding and intra-graben faults that controlled hydrothermal fluid pathways. When scoring lithology, cells within 1,700 m of NW-NNE normal faults in Eocene volcanic terrain should receive the highest scores.

## Scoring Decision Logic

When evaluating a grid cell, follow this sequence:

1. **Identify the rock type** from spatial context (Macrostrat or geological survey data).
2. **Match to Washington province** — Is this cell in the Okanogan Highlands? North Cascades? Columbia Plateau? Province context adjusts baseline expectations.
3. **Determine the applicable deposit model**:
   - Volcanic rocks (andesite, dacite, tuff) in Eocene graben settings → evaluate as **epithermal**
   - Metamorphic rocks (schist, phyllite, amphibolite) in North Cascades → evaluate as **orogenic**
   - Carbonates near intrusive contacts → evaluate as **skarn**
   - If ambiguous, score for the most applicable model and note the reasoning.
4. **Look up the score** from the rubric tables above.
5. **Apply modifiers**:
   - Interlayered/heterolithic sequences: +0.05 to +0.10 (better permeability and competency contrast)
   - Near known intrusive contact: +0.05 for skarn potential
   - Under cover (CRBG, glacial till, young sediments): use confidence penalty approach — score the underlying lithology normally but reduce confidence by 0.15–0.25 and note "under cover" in evidence
6. **Assign confidence** (see calibration below).
7. **Write evidence** explaining the score — cite specific formation names, deposit model reasoning, and any modifiers applied.

### Washington-Specific Deposit Model Weights for Gold

When a cell could be scored under multiple models, weight them:
- Epithermal: 0.55 (dominant model for Washington gold)
- Orogenic: 0.35 (second most productive)
- Skarn: 0.10 (minor contributor)

## Confidence Calibration

| Level | Range | When to Assign |
|---|---|---|
| Very High | 0.85–1.0 | Detailed geological mapping (1:24k+) available; specific formation name known; multiple confirming data sources. |
| High | 0.70–0.85 | Standard geological survey data; formation identified; moderate spatial resolution. |
| Medium | 0.50–0.70 | Regional-scale mapping only; lithology known but age or composition uncertain; Macrostrat data with moderate coverage. |
| Low | 0.25–0.50 | Sparse data; lithology inferred from regional context; under significant cover; "undifferentiated" descriptions. |
| Very Low | 0.0–0.25 | No data; pure inference from regional geological models; highly speculative. |

**Confidence penalties to apply:**
- "Undifferentiated" or "composition uncertain" lithology: -0.15
- Under cover (basalt, till, sediment): -0.15 to -0.25 depending on cover thickness
- Broad age range (>50 Ma uncertainty): -0.10
- Inferred from regional mapping only (no measured section): -0.20

## Common Interpretation Pitfalls

**Do not score metamorphic rocks high for epithermal gold.** Schist, phyllite, and amphibolite are orogenic gold hosts, not epithermal. Epithermal score for metamorphic rocks should be 0.00–0.10 unless there's explicit evidence of a younger volcanic/hydrothermal overprint.

**Do not treat CRBG cover as "unknown."** CRBG is a definitive negative indicator. Score it very low (0.02–0.08). You may note in evidence that mineralized basement could exist underneath, but this is speculative and should not inflate the cell score. The confidence for "what's under the basalt" should be very low.

**Do not ignore interlayering.** "Interbedded andesite and sandstone" or "mica schist with amphibolite layers" are *more* favorable than either pure lithology alone. Interlayering creates permeability contrasts that localize ore deposition. Apply +0.05–0.10 heterogeneity bonus.

**Do not score based on age alone.** A Cenozoic basalt is still unfavorable; a Cenozoic andesite in a graben setting is highly favorable. Evaluate composition first, then structural/tectonic setting, then age.

**Distinguish between the Sanpoil Volcanics and generic "Tertiary volcanics."** The Sanpoil is specifically Eocene calc-alkaline in a graben setting — proven gold host. Generic "Tertiary volcanic rocks" in western Washington are typically barren younger Cascade arc volcanics. Context matters enormously.

**Metamorphic grade matters for orogenic gold.** Greenschist to lower amphibolite facies is optimal. Upper amphibolite and granulite facies rocks are generally too high-grade — reduced porosity, permeability, and reactivity. Score these lower (0.10–0.30).

**Washington lacks major porphyry systems.** Unlike the southwestern US, Washington's Cretaceous subduction geometry did not produce large porphyry copper-gold deposits. Do not apply porphyry deposit models when scoring Washington lithologies. Epithermal and orogenic models are dominant.

**Eocene intrusive rocks are NOT gold hosts despite being Eocene-aged.** The WofE analysis (OF01-501) tested all Eocene intrusive units (Eig, Eimd, Eiqm — combined >580 km²) and found **zero** training sites. Do not score Eocene granites, granodiorites, or diorites high for epithermal gold just because they are the same age as the Sanpoil Volcanics. The extrusive volcanic and volcaniclastic rocks are the hosts, not the intrusives.

**Use the 150 m lithologic buffer.** The WofE optimum buffer for lithologic units is 150 m. Cells within 150 m of favorable lithology boundaries should be scored as if they contained that lithology (mineralization extends slightly beyond mapped contacts). Beyond 150 m, the lithologic predictor weakens rapidly.

**92% of NE Washington is non-permissive for epithermal gold.** The WofE model rigorously demonstrates that epithermal gold is concentrated in a very small fraction of the landscape. Do not inflate scores for cells outside Eocene volcanic terrain just because they are "in NE Washington." The non-permissive classification (P<0.000167) covers 92% of the study area with zero training sites.
