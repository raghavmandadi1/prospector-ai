# Historical Agent — Gold Knowledge Base (Washington State)

You are scoring grid cells in Washington state for gold prospecting potential based on historical mining evidence. This reference gives you the context to interpret mining records, production data, claim patterns, and exploration maturity — and translate them into defensible scores.

The single most important thing to understand: **most Washington gold mines closed for economic reasons, not because the ore ran out.** This means historical mining activity is a strong positive signal, and districts with past production almost certainly have remaining mineralization.

---

## Washington Gold Mining Timeline

Understanding *when* and *why* mining happened helps you interpret the records you'll see in the data.

**1855–1880s (Early Placer Era):** First gold found in Peshastin Creek (1855). Scattered placer rushes on Skykomish, Swauk, and Colville-area streams. Production was small and poorly recorded. Miners could only find surface gold — outcropping veins and stream gravels. Anything below a few feet of cover was invisible to them.

**1889–1920 (Lode Mining Boom):** The golden era. Monte Cristo district founded 1889, Republic district organized 1896. Eureka Mine at Republic opened 1900 and became Washington's largest gold producer. Railroad access enabled ore shipment to Tacoma and Seattle smelters. Peak statewide production of 2–2.5 Moz during this period. Shaft depths reached 800–1,300 ft at Republic, 400–500 ft at Monte Cristo, 400–600 ft at Blewett.

**1920–1933 (Margin Squeeze):** Gold price fixed at $20.67/oz while mining costs rose post-WWI. Smaller mines closed 1925–1932. The 1893 silver panic had already devastated polymetallic operations. Operating margins fell below 5% for most mines.

**1933 (Gold Confiscation):** Roosevelt's Executive Order 6102 required surrender of private gold at $20.67/oz. Devastating to small operators. The price was then reset to $35/oz in 1934, but cost structures had already broken most marginal operations.

**1942 (WWII L-208 Order):** War Production Board Order L-208 shut down all non-essential gold mining. Every major Washington gold mine ceased operations by end of 1942. This was an abrupt, externally-forced closure — not gradual depletion. No Washington gold mine successfully reopened after the war.

**1945–1970 (Permanent Decline):** Gold price stayed fixed at $35/oz until 1968. Insufficient to justify the capital needed to rebuild decayed infrastructure. Mills were scrapped, shafts flooded, roads deteriorated. The workforce dispersed permanently.

**1970–Present (Exploration Without Production):** Gold price floated ($875/oz peak in 1980, $2,000+ by 2020s). Junior exploration companies explored Republic district in the 1980s–1990s with some drilling. Wilderness designations (Glacier Peak, 1964; Henry M. Jackson, 1984) locked up Monte Cristo area. Environmental permitting (5–10 year timelines) blocks commercial reopening. Current production is essentially zero — hobby placer mining only.

## Why Mines Closed: District-by-District

This is critical context for scoring. A cell near a mine that closed because the ore ran out is different from one near a mine shut down by a government order with ore still in the ground.

### Republic District (Ferry County)
**Closure cause: Economics + WWII L-208, NOT exhaustion.**
The Eureka Mine's shaft reached 1,300 ft, but most productive stopes only extended to 400–900 ft. When the fixed gold price couldn't cover rising costs at depth, deeper mining stopped — not because the vein ended. The L-208 order in 1942 terminated operations permanently. Post-war estimates suggest 500,000–1,000,000 oz of ore remain unworked. Modern drilling in the 1980s–1990s reported encouraging assays at depth. This district has very high remaining potential.

**USGS OF01-501 Training Sites in Republic Area** (43 of 50 total epithermal training sites):
Named producers: Knob Hill mine (Large production, MRDS SP00084), Lone Pine (Moderate), Quilp/Imperator/Eureka, Ben Hur, Blacktail/Hope, El Caliph, Insurgent, Last Chance, Little Cove, Mountain Lion, North Sanpoil, Princess Maude, Surprise, Tom Thumb. Most cluster tightly at lat ~48.65°N, lon ~118.75°W. Sites with "Large" production designation: Knob Hill and K-2 Mine. The vast majority of sites had "Small" or "No production" — indicating that even marginally developed prospects cluster in the same favorable terrain.

**K-2 Mine** (lat 48.866°N, lon 118.668°W): A significant discovery in the Toroda Creek Graben, classified as "Large" production. Located north of the main Republic district, demonstrating that favorable terrain extends beyond the historically-worked Republic core.

**Kettle Mine** (lat 48.879°N, lon 118.626°W): Another "Large" producer, notable because it has the *lowest* posterior probability (0.0007) of any training site — it's buried under Quaternary deposits. Demonstrates that cover masks otherwise favorable ground.

### Monte Cristo District (Snohomish County)
**Closure cause: Infrastructure destruction + access + economics.**
The railroad through the Cascade corridor was repeatedly destroyed by avalanches and floods (catastrophic damage in 1910). Freight costs escalated. The working season was only 6–7 months due to alpine conditions. Mines reached 400–500 ft before pumping costs became prohibitive. Higher-grade stopes were partially depleted, but lower-grade zones and depth extensions remain untested. Modern development is blocked by Wilderness Area designation (1984).

### Blewett / Peshastin District (Chelan County)
**Closure cause: Moderate grades + placer exhaustion + no railroad.**
Ore grades were lower (0.15–0.40 oz/ton) than Republic or Monte Cristo. Easy placer gravels in Peshastin Creek were depleted by 1910. Without rail access, ore transportation costs were high. Most lode mines reached only 400–600 ft. Depth potential exists but was never tested. This district is notably under-explored by modern methods — no geochemical surveys, no geophysics, no modern drilling.

### Liberty / Swauk District (Kittitas County)
**Closure cause: Placer exhaustion; lode potential limited.**
A placer-dominated district. Stream gravels were worked out by the 1920s. Attempts at lode mining found only moderate grades (0.20–0.40 oz/ton) without large high-grade zones. Remaining potential is low for primary gold.

### Colville-Metaline District (Stevens & Pend Oreille Counties)
**Closure cause: WWII L-208 + shift to strategic metals.**
Multiple small-to-medium mines operated, benefiting from proximity to Trail, BC smelters. Post-WWII focus shifted to tungsten and molybdenum. Most mines never reopened for gold. This district is very poorly explored by modern methods and may have the highest untapped potential relative to its historical production.

**OF01-501 Training Sites**: First Thought Mine (lat 48.884°N, lon 118.161°W, MRDS M060170/M056476, "Small" production, Comstock epithermal type 25c), Hidden Treasure (M056495, "No production"), Nest Egg (M056501, "No production"). These are located in the First Thought Graben — the easternmost of the four NE Washington grabens — and are hosted by Eocene volcanic rocks analogous to the Republic district.

### Interpreting Historical Grades

Historical fire assay reports are systematically inflated compared to modern analytical methods. Apply these corrections:

- **Fire assay "average" ore grades**: Discount 15–30% to estimate true bulk milling grade. Example: Eureka reported 0.45 oz/ton average → actual ~0.30–0.35 oz/ton.
- **"Selected shipping ore" grades**: These were hand-picked high-grade samples (1.0–5.0 oz/ton reported). Assume only 30–50% of mined ore was at this grade.
- **Government reports (USGS, Bureau of Mines)**: More conservative and reliable than company reports, which had investor-marketing incentives.

## Exploration Maturity by District

This tells you how to interpret *absence* of historical records. No mines in an area might mean "no gold" or "nobody looked yet."

### Republic — Well-explored (surface), partially explored (depth)
Main veins mapped and worked. But: no modern geochemical soil surveys, no induced polarization (IP) surveys, no hyperspectral mapping, minimal deep drilling. Adjacent parallel veins barely tested. Depth below 1,000 ft largely unexplored. **Remaining gaps are at depth and on parallel structures.**

The USGS assessment model (OF01-501) shows that 33 of 50 training sites fall in "favorable + consistent claims" areas — the highest-activity category. However, another 16 sites fall in favorable areas with NO claims activity (likely private land). This means private land parcels within the Republic Graben are as geologically prospective as claimed public land — the lack of claims is a land-status artifact, not a geological signal.

### Monte Cristo — Moderately explored; access-restricted
Good surface geological mapping (USGS 1:24k published). Some USGS reconnaissance stream sediment surveys in 1980s identified anomalies. But: Wilderness Area designation (1984) blocks new drilling, IP, soil sampling. **Depth potential is real but legally inaccessible for now.**

### Blewett — Poorly explored by modern methods; highest upside
No modern geochemical surveys whatsoever. No IP, no hyperspectral, no modern drilling. Historic placer success proves gold is present; lode sources upstream were never systematically characterized. Good road access (WA-97). **This is the most under-explored district relative to its potential in Washington.**

### Colville-Metaline — Very poorly explored; highest untapped potential
Almost no modern exploration of any kind. Historical focus was on base metals (Pb, Zn, W, Mo), with gold as a byproduct. District geology is poorly understood. **Modern survey coverage is nearly zero — any discovery would be new.**

### Liberty/Swauk — Well-explored for placer; lode potential limited
Placer deposits thoroughly worked. Lode potential appears genuinely limited by grade. Low priority for modern exploration.

## Data Sources and Their Quirks

### USGS MRDS (Mineral Resources Data System)
Washington has approximately 800–1,200 gold-related MRDS records (350–500 with gold as primary commodity). Weight records by development status:

| Status | Weight | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| Significant Producer (>100 koz) | 1.0 | Proven large mineral system. Strongest indicator. |
| Producer (>10 koz) | 0.90 | Proven deposit. Strong district fertility signal. |
| Developed Prospect | 0.65–0.75 | Known mineralization, partially explored, not mined. Mineral system worked here. |
| Prospect | 0.40–0.50 | Evidence of mineralization, limited development. Quality varies widely. |
| Occurrence | 0.20–0.30 | Mineralization present but significance unclear. Often from regional surveys. |

**MRDS location accuracy problems**: 25–35% of records from 1970s–1980s digitization have coordinate errors >500 m, some >5 km. Older records used GLO descriptions or town-name references, not GPS. Treat individual MRDS points as approximate (±500 m to ±2 km) but treat clusters as meaningful spatial patterns.

### BLM Mining Claims
Washington has approximately 15,000–20,000 active mining claims on federal lands. Concentration: 40–45% in NE Washington (Ferry, Okanogan, Pend Oreille counties), 25–30% in North Cascades, 15–20% in central WA.

**Interpreting claim patterns:**
- High density (>100 claims per 10 km²): Historically productive district. Proven mineralization. Examples: Republic core, Blewett area.
- Medium density (20–100 per 10 km²): Secondary exploration zone or district fringe. Often contains overlooked prospects.
- Scattered (<20 per 10 km²): Frontier or low-priority area. If in geologically favorable terrain, may indicate emerging interest.
- **Active claims** mean someone is currently investing money because they believe gold is there.
- **Lapsed claims** don't mean "no gold" — they often reflect commodity price cycles. Major lapse waves occurred during 2015–2016 gold price dip; re-staking accelerated 2020–2023.

### Mining Claims Activity Patterns (USGS OF01-501 Assessment Model)

The USGS analyzed BLM lode mining claims at four snapshots (1980, 1985, 1990, 1996) across NE Washington and defined three activity categories:

- **Historic activity**: Claims present in 1980 or 1985 but not later — indicates past interest that has waned.
- **Current activity**: Claims present in 1990 or 1996 — indicates ongoing interest.
- **Consistent activity**: Claims present in BOTH historic AND current periods — the strongest indicator. Areas with consistent claims have sustained, multi-decade interest.

**Key findings for scoring:**

| Activity Pattern | Combined with Favorable Lithology | Assessment | OF01-501 Training Sites |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consistent claims + Favorable | **HIGH activity predicted** | 33 of 50 sites (66%) | Score 0.80–0.95 |
| No claims + Favorable | **Moderate activity** | 16 of 50 sites (32%) — likely on private land | Score 0.65–0.85 |
| Historic/Current claims + Favorable | **High activity predicted** | Included in high/moderate | Score 0.70–0.90 |
| Any claims + Permissive | **Low-to-moderate activity** | 1 site (Kettle mine) | Score 0.35–0.55 |
| No claims + Permissive | **Little or no activity** | 0 sites | Score 0.15–0.35 |
| Any/None + Non-permissive | **No activity predicted** | 0 sites | Score 0.00–0.15 |

**Caveats on claims data:**
- Mining claims only exist on public (federal) land. The 16 training sites with no claims activity are likely on private land — the absence of claims does NOT mean absence of mineral potential.
- 32% of gold exploration projects in the NE Washington grabens (1985–1997) targeted gold in settings other than epithermal. Claims data doesn't distinguish deposit types.
- The checkerboard pattern of claimed sections in the Republic and Toroda Creek grabens reflects the interleaving of public and private land, not inconsistent mineralization.

### GLO (General Land Office) Records
GLO surveys in Washington were conducted 1860s–1910s. Surveyors noted mineral observations as they walked section lines. Their terminology needs translation:

- "Gold bearing" / "gold color" = Visual observation of gold or pyrite (sometimes confused)
- "Quartz veins" = Most common mineral notation; follows major fault zones
- "Prospects" / "diggings" = Active mining at survey time
- "Mineral land" = Legal classification for land with mineral potential

**Reliability**: HIGH for confirming vein presence. MODERATE for extent. LOW for predicting value or grade (surveyors did not assay). GLO notes in Ferry, Snohomish, Kittitas, and Chelan counties have the highest mineral notation frequency and correlate well with known districts.

### Placer Gold Sites (USGS OF01-501 Predictor Theme)

67 placer gold sites were documented across NE Washington and used as a predictor theme in the WofE analysis. The spatial correlation between placer sites and epithermal gold deposits is significant — the optimum buffer distance is **4,000 m** (the largest of the three predictor themes).

**County distribution**: Ferry County (21 sites), Okanogan County (20 sites), Stevens County (19 sites), Pend Oreille County (5 sites), Boundary County ID (2 sites).

**How to use for scoring**: A documented placer gold site within 4 km of a grid cell is a positive historical indicator — it confirms gold was being eroded and transported from nearby bedrock sources. Placer sites downstream of Eocene volcanic terrain are especially significant because they may indicate undiscovered epithermal lode sources. Key clusters: Sanpoil River placers (Ferry County, 10+ sites along river), Columbia River bar placers (Stevens County), Similkameen River placers (Okanogan County).

**Limitations**: Placer sites indicate gold presence in drainage but do not precisely locate the bedrock source. The source may be several km upstream and at higher elevation than the placer deposit.

### Washington AML (Abandoned Mine Lands)
Approximately 600–900 formally documented sites (WA DNR inventory). Estimated 1,200–2,000 total including undocumented sites. Four counties contain the majority: Ferry (120–150 sites), Pend Oreille (100–130), Snohomish (80–110), Chelan (60–90).

**Location accuracy varies**: ~30–40% within ±100 m, ~40–50% within ±500 m, ~10–20% within ±2–5 km, ~5–10% have errors >10 km (county centroids or generic references). Treat AML data the same as MRDS — individual points are approximate, but clusters are meaningful.

## Scoring Rubric

### Assay Primacy — Apply Before All Other Rubrics

Reported assay, grade, and production values are the strongest class of historical
evidence and take precedence over proximity-based scoring. When a database record
in or adjacent to a cell carries actual numbers, score from the assay, not the district:

| Assay Evidence in/adjacent to Cell | Score Range | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ore-grade lode assays (≥5 g/t Au, or historical "high-grade" production records) | 0.85–0.95 | High | Direct proof of mineralization at this location. Quote the values in evidence. |
| Sub-ore but strongly anomalous assays (0.5–5 g/t Au; Au_ppb ≥ 500 in rock/soil) | 0.65–0.85 | High | Mineralized system confirmed; economics, not geology, was the limit. |
| Anomalous pathfinder assays (elevated As/Sb/Hg with trace Au) | 0.45–0.65 | Medium-High | Halo of a possible nearby system. |
| Documented production tonnage/oz without surviving assay detail | 0.70–0.90 | High | Production is itself an assay proxy — score per District Proximity top rows. |
| Trace/background values from systematic sampling | 0.10–0.25 | Medium-High | A real measurement of "not much here" — more meaningful than no data. |

Rules of application:
- Assay-backed scores are NOT capped by the District Proximity table; a high-grade
  assay in an unnamed area outranks an assay-free cell inside a famous district.
- District proximity WITHOUT any assay or production backing caps at ~0.6.
- Apply the ±1 km MRDS location-uncertainty caveat: let a strong assay influence
  the immediately adjacent cells at reduced strength, not just its host cell.
- Always quote the numeric values and the source record name in evidence strings.
- **Do not infer whether a nearby occurrence was assay-backed or production-backed.**
  You are now given authoritative boolean flags for both. Look them up — see the
  next section, "WA DNR Record Flags".

### WA DNR Record Flags — Hard Lookup, Not Inference

The occurrence records you receive per cell come from **WA DNR / WGS Mines & Minerals**
(`Gold_Silver_Locations`, 1,467 sites; `Metallic_Mineral_Locations`, 1,847 sites) and carry
machine-readable fields. These are compiled facts from the state survey, not your inference:

| Field | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `assays` | bool | `true` ⇔ the WA DNR record notes that assay data exists for the site |
| `production` | bool | `true` ⇔ the record notes recorded production from the site |
| `accuracy_class` | enum | `survey` / `topo` / `derived` / `variable` / `district_centroid` / `unknown` |
| `ore_minerals`, `gangue` | text | observed mineralogy at the site |
| `district` | text | mapped mining district membership (68 districts statewide) |
| `doc_count`, `docs` | int, list | rows in `Metallic_Minerals_Scanned_Documents` (107,739 statewide); up to 3 titles, newest first |
| `comments` | text | free text — **the one place actual numeric grades sometimes appear** |

**What the flags do and do not say.** `assays: true` means *a measurement exists somewhere in the
literature*, usually in a scanned bulletin. It is **not** a grade, and it is certainly not "ore
grade". `production: true` means the site produced, **not how much** — production amounts exist at
district scale (`district.production_amount`), not per site. Treating either flag as a grade is the
fastest way to produce a confidently wrong high score.

**Calibrate against the base rates.** Of the 1,467 gold/silver sites, 649 (44%) have `assays: true`
and 450 (31%) have `production: true`. Roughly a third of all sites carry a production flag, so
`production: true` is *common, not extraordinary* — it does not by itself justify a 0.90.

#### Score bands keyed off the two booleans

For a flagged site **in or immediately adjacent to the cell** (within about one cell width). Apply
the distance decay from the District Proximity Scoring table below on top of these, and the accuracy
modifier from the next subsection.

| `production` | `assays` | Evidence class | Score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `true` | `true` | Past producer with surviving assay documentation — the strongest historical class available | 0.80–0.92 | 0.70–0.85 |
| `true` | `false` | Recorded production, no assay compilation. Production is itself an assay proxy. | 0.70–0.85 | 0.65–0.80 |
| `false` | `true` | Chemically characterised prospect that never produced — mineralization confirmed, economics or depth was the limit | 0.55–0.72 | 0.60–0.75 |
| `false` | `false` | Occurrence noted, nothing measured, nothing mined | 0.25–0.45 | 0.45–0.60 |

Two escalations remain available above these bands, and only these two:

- **Numeric grades quoted in `comments`.** If the text carries oz/ton, g/t, ppm or percentages,
  score from the numbers under the Assay Primacy table above and quote them verbatim. This is the
  only route to the 0.85–0.95 band from a single record.
- **Multiple production-flagged sites** clustered around the cell, at least one `survey`/`topo`
  located — a district-scale producing pattern rather than one site.

#### `accuracy_class` modifies the DISTANCE argument, not the evidence class

The flags tell you *what* was found. `accuracy_class` tells you *how well anyone knows where*.

| `accuracy_class` | n (gold/silver layer) | Positional tolerance | Effect on your argument |
|---|---|---|---|
| `survey` | 45 | ±50–100 m | Can anchor a single cell at 500–1000 m resolution |
| `topo` | 244 | ±100–500 m | Can anchor a cell; note 213 of the 244 are the weaker "generally from 7.5-minute map" variant |
| `derived` | 237 | ±0.4–2 km | Spreads over 1–4 cells. A PLSS quarter-quarter is a 400 m square; a bare section is 1,609 m. |
| `variable` | **917** | **≥ ±2 km, unbounded** | **Neighbourhood-scale statement only.** Never a single-cell spike. |
| `district_centroid` | 24 | tens of km | **Not a site.** Never anchors a distance argument at all — fall back to district membership. |
| `unknown` | — | treat as `variable` | |

Rules:

- **62.5% of the gold/silver sites are `variable`.** An evidence class of "past producer with
  assays" on a `variable`-accuracy site is a strong statement about the *district* and a weak one
  about the *cell*. Spread it; do not spike it.
- A flag-driven score above **0.75** requires at least one `survey` or `topo` site in the supporting
  set. Otherwise cap at 0.70 and say why in the evidence.
- If `accuracy_class == "district_centroid"`, discard the reported distance entirely and score from
  district membership. These 24 records are district centres masquerading as sites.

#### How this changes the MRDS caveat

The MRDS positional-accuracy warning elsewhere in this file still stands — MRDS points really are
approximate. What has changed is that **`accuracy_class` now tells you which records are
survey-grade, so a blanket ±1 km caveat over everything is no longer the right response.** A blanket
caveat throws away the 45 GPS-located sites along with the 917 variable ones. Apply the
per-record tolerance from the table above instead, and keep the blanket caveat only for records that
arrive without an `accuracy_class`.

#### Mineralogy and document count

- `ore_minerals` / `gangue` are real observations and are your check that the nearby style is
  actually gold-bearing. Arsenopyrite, adularia, chalcedonic quartz and bladed calcite support a
  gold interpretation; a **galena–sphalerite-dominant** assemblage is the Metaline base-metal style
  and scores 0.10–0.25 for gold regardless of its flags — see "Polymetallic Associations" below.
- `doc_count` is corroboration of **existence and significance, not of grade**: a site written about
  in five scanned bulletins was real and was thought to matter. Treat `doc_count ≥ 3` as +0.03, and
  never as a substitute for the assay and production flags.

Cite `WA_DNR_WGS_Mines_and_Minerals` in `data_sources_used` whenever any of these fields carried
your score, and quote the flag values and `accuracy_class` in the evidence string so a reader can
check the lookup: e.g. `"'Lone Pine' 0.6 km, production=true, assays=true, accuracy_class=topo —
past producer with surviving assay documentation; flags read directly from the WA DNR record, not
inferred."`

### Exploration Maturity Modifier

Adjust scores based on how thoroughly an area has been explored:

| Exploration Maturity | Modifier | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Well-explored, productive (Republic core) | No adjustment | Records are representative; what you see is what's there (at surface). |
| Well-explored, unproductive | -0.10 to -0.15 | If thorough exploration found nothing, absence is meaningful. |
| Poorly explored (Blewett, Colville-Metaline) | +0.05 to +0.10 | Absence of records less meaningful; area may have been overlooked. |
| Exploration-restricted (Wilderness, tribal land) | +0.05 to +0.10 | Legal restrictions, not geological assessment, explain gaps. Note restriction in evidence. |

When historical mines reached only shallow depths for economic reasons (not geological exhaustion), their vicinity has untapped depth potential:

| District | Depth Tested | Depth Potential Rating | Score Modifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Republic | 400–1,300 ft | VERY HIGH — ore continues below tested depths | +0.05 to +0.10 |
| Monte Cristo | 400–500 ft | MODERATE-HIGH — depth largely untested | +0.03 to +0.08 |
| Blewett | 400–600 ft | MODERATE — deeper extensions plausible | +0.03 to +0.05 |

### Historical Technology Limitations Modifier

Early miners (pre-1920s) could not find or exploit certain deposit types. Areas explored only before these technology thresholds have untested potential:

- **Before cyanide leaching (adopted ~1890s):** Low-grade disseminated gold was invisible. Districts explored only in the placer era may have lode deposits that were never recognized.
- **Before flotation (adopted ~1920s):** Complex sulfide ores with gold locked in pyrite/arsenopyrite couldn't be economically processed. Old tailings and "waste rock" may contain recoverable gold by modern methods.
- **Before geophysics (IP, magnetics, gravity — post-1950s):** Blind deposits (under cover, at depth) couldn't be detected. Areas with only pre-1950s exploration may have completely untested subsurface targets.
- **Before geochemistry (soil/stream sediment surveys — post-1960s):** Subtle halos around buried deposits were undetectable. Districts without modern geochemical surveys (Blewett, Colville-Metaline) have a genuine exploration gap.

If a cell falls in a district explored only before a relevant technology threshold, note this in evidence and apply +0.03 to +0.05 as an "untested potential" modifier.

## USGS Deposit Type Classifications (OF01-501 Training Set)

The 50 epithermal training sites are classified by USGS deposit model:

| Model Code | Model Name | Count | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25a | Hot spring Au-Ag | 26 | Shallow, near-surface epithermal. No significant production for most. Includes disseminated and stockwork mineralization. |
| 25b | Creede epithermal veins | 4 | Intermediate-depth vein systems. Examples: Zalla M, American Flag, Silver Bell. Cluster in Toroda Creek area. |
| 25c | Comstock epithermal veins | 20 | Deep, well-defined vein systems. All significant producers are 25c: Knob Hill, Lone Pine, Quilp, First Thought. |

**Scoring implication**: When MRDS records include a deposit model code, use it to assess significance. Comstock-type (25c) deposits are the most economically important — they form well-defined veins with consistent grades. Hot spring-type (25a) deposits are more numerous but mostly small. An area with multiple 25c-classified deposits should score higher than one with only 25a occurrences.

**Production size distribution**: Of 50 sites — Large: 3 (Knob Hill, K-2, Kettle), Moderate: 1 (Lone Pine), Small: 20, No production: 26. The fact that 26 of 50 training sites have no recorded production means the WofE model identifies favorable terrain even where mining hasn't occurred — the geological predictors work independently of production history.

## Polymetallic Associations

Washington gold districts have different metal associations that affect interpretation:

**Republic (Au-Ag epithermal):** Au is primary. Ag is a significant byproduct (Au:Ag ratio 1:5 to 1:20). As and Sb are pathfinder elements. When you see MRDS records coded "Au, Ag" in Ferry County, these are genuine gold deposits.

**Monte Cristo (Au-Ag-Cu polymetallic):** Complex. Some mines produced more copper than gold by value. Au:Ag ratio 1:10 to 1:30, with 0.5–2% Cu in deeper zones. Records coded "Au, Ag, Cu" should be scored positively for gold, but understand that deeper levels trend toward Cu-rich.

**Metaline (Pb-Zn-Ag with Au byproduct):** Gold is a trace byproduct (0.01–0.1 oz/ton). Records coded "Pb, Zn, Ag, Au" in Pend Oreille County are primarily base-metal skarns. Score these LOW for gold potential (0.10–0.25). The Au is real but not economically recoverable as a primary target.

**Rule:** When you see a multi-commodity MRDS record, check which commodity was actually the primary production target. "Au, Ag, Cu" from Monte Cristo is a gold indicator. "Pb, Zn, Ag, Au" from Metaline is not.

## Common Interpretation Pitfalls

**Do not assume "no records" means "no gold."** In poorly explored districts (Blewett, Colville-Metaline), absence of historical records reflects exploration maturity, not geological reality. Check whether the area was ever systematically explored before scoring it low.

**Do not treat all MRDS points as equally reliable locations.** Older records can be mislocated by kilometers. Use cluster patterns, not individual points, for spatial analysis. Add ±1 km uncertainty to any single historical mine location.

**Do not penalize districts closed by WWII L-208.** The closure was a government order, not a geological verdict. Republic, Monte Cristo, and Colville-Metaline all had remaining ore when they were forced to shut down.

**Do not equate shallow mining with shallow ore.** Most Washington mines reached only 400–900 ft because of 1920s economics, not because veins terminated. The vertical extent of most ore systems is untested.

**Do not discount old records because they're old.** A mine that produced 100,000 oz in 1910 is just as meaningful as one that produced 100,000 oz in 2010. The geology hasn't changed. What matters is whether the production record is reliable (prefer government reports over company promotional materials).

**Do distinguish placer from lode records.** Placer production (Liberty/Swauk) proves gold exists in the drainage but doesn't precisely locate the source. Lode production (Republic, Monte Cristo, Blewett) directly identifies mineralized rock. Weight lode records higher for cell-level scoring.

**Watch for location accuracy degradation.** Ferry County records from the 1890s–1920s are better located than average because of the concentrated district activity. Scattered records in remote North Cascades areas may have 2–5 km errors.

**Do not treat absence of mining claims as absence of mineral potential.** The USGS assessment model (OF01-501) found that 16 of 50 known epithermal gold sites have NO associated mining claims — because they're on private land. Mining claims only exist on federal land. In a checkerboard land ownership pattern (common in NE Washington), claimed and unclaimed sections may have identical geology.

**Do not limit "Republic district" to the town of Republic.** The 50 USGS training sites span from lat 48.56°N to 48.99°N and lon 118.06°W to 118.90°W — a much larger area than just the Republic townsite. The K-2 Mine and Kettle Mine are >20 km north of Republic but are geologically equivalent epithermal deposits in the Toroda Creek Graben. Score the full extent of all four NE Washington grabens (Republic, Toroda Creek, Keller, First Thought), not just the historic Republic core.

**Use placer sites as a 4 km proximity indicator.** The WofE analysis showed that placer gold sites have an optimum spatial correlation with epithermal deposits at 4,000 m buffer distance. A documented placer within 4 km is a historically-derived indicator of nearby lode gold — treat it as positive evidence even if no lode mines are documented nearby.
